**Plain script (read top to bottom):**

You know this tune, but without a tab your hands don’t know where to go. Let’s fix that, on Silent Night, using just the high E string.

First, hum the first four bars. Bars one and two are the same: only three bars to work out.

Step one: find home, the note where the song feels finished. Hum the last note of the song, then slide up until the guitar matches your voice. Fret ten, a D. That’s home.

Step two: bright or dark. Play a D chord, then D minor. One finger moves. Hum the tune over each one. One fits. The other doesn’t. Silent Night is the bright one: major.

Step three: walk down from home. Ten, nine, seven, five, three, two, zero, and twelve above home. These frets belong to the key. Only these frets sound right. Skip the others. The search gets much smaller.

Step four: pick it note by note. Sing the first words: Silent night. Try fret five. Up one step: fret six? Not quite. Not in the key. Fret seven. Back to five, then down to two. Bar two repeats. Bar three leaps up to fret twelve. Fret nine leans up to home. Bar four: ten, ten, five. Now you have your own tab.

Find home. Bright or dark. Key frets. Pick by ear. Which song can’t you find tabs for? Comment it.

---

**Do not say:** "easy", exclamation-mark energy, or any number that is not on screen. Say “Not quite” flat and kind — it is a normal part of picking a tune, not a failure (docs/voice.md).
